\section{Summary of Results}\label{sec:summary}

We return to the two research questions from the introduction.

\textbf{RQ1: How can LLM-based characters maintain narrative coherence and stay on-story when users interrupt or ask unexpected questions?} By separating deciding from performing. With a Director that tracks the current beat and steers every turn back to it, participants rated the story as far more coherent (3.93 vs.\ 1.71) and the character as far better at staying on track when they tried to derail it (4.42 vs.\ 1.58), both $p \le 0.002$, while the character's voice was unaffected. The LLM-as-Judge evaluation shows the same advantage, also for a second character.

\textbf{RQ2: How can LLM-based characters plan and execute complete narratives within fixed time budgets?} By monitoring time outside the model and telling the Director when to compress or skip lower-importance beats. Under a 3-minute limit, the Time-Constrained Director reached a well-paced ending in 71\% of rounds, compared with 86\% of unconstrained rounds being cut short, and its endings were rated as more satisfying (\pval{0.016}), without a measurable loss on the other measures.

The main findings of the user study are:

\begin{itemize}
\item Adding a director helps, clearly, on coherence and interruption handling -- the two strongest, most individually-consistent effects in the study (both $p \le 0.002$ in Story 1).

\item The failure modes differ qualitatively, not just numerically: baseline lets the story stall completely when interrupted; director keeps going.

\item Time-constrained vs.\ director: the unconstrained director round rarely reaches a real ending (86\% cut short), while the time-constrained round usually does (71\% well-paced) and is rated as more satisfying at the ending (\pval{0.016}).

\item Character consistency is solved across all three conditions (4.00--4.29) -- what varies is narrative progress and how interruptions are handled, not whether Olaf stays Olaf.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

Our setup has several limits.
\begin{itemize}
  \item We test only two characters, Olaf and Rocket Raccoon, and mostly on Disney stories, so we cannot say how far the results carry over to other characters and genres.
  \item All variants use the same, small language model, GPT-4o-mini. Larger models may be less prone to drift.
  \item The automated evaluation relies on a single judge model for most comparisons, and scripted user inputs cannot react to what the character says.
  \item The beat generator and the lenient Director have not been tested thoroughly.
  \item The user study has a small sample, only 14 participants who completed it, and limited time per participant.
\end{itemize}
