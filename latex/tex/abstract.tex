
Interactive narratives are fundamental to educational games, dialogue systems, and generative entertainment, yet contemporary dialogue systems excel at fluency while lacking explicit narrative goals. When users ask unexpected questions or deviate from the intended story arc, language models typically drift off-story, losing narrative coherence and failing to reach intended climaxes within bounded time windows.

We address this limitation through an integrated system combining three mechanisms: (1) beat-based story representation that decomposes narratives into discrete plot points; (2) director mode steering that monitors semantic deviation and dynamically guides the character back on-story; and (3) a temporal monitor that adapts story pacing under time constraints.

LLM-as-Judge and controlled user studies show director mode substantially improves narrative coherence and resists user derailment. Temporal constraints enable reliable story completion within fixed budgets while resisting user derailments. Our findings demonstrate that explicit narrative structure combined with real-time steering preserves both story coherence and conversational fluency.
